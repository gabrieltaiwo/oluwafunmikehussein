When a regulatory inspection, shareholder challenge, or commercial dispute reaches a board years after a decision was taken, recollection is rarely sufficient. Under Nigerian company law and regulatory practice, the statutory minute book serves as primary prima facie evidence of what the board considered, debated, and resolved.

Too often, minute books suffer from one of two extremes: either they read like a verbatim transcript that obscures the rationale behind a resolution, or they record only bare outcomes with no indication that directors exercised independent judgement.

## Recording Deliberation Without Over-Scripting

Effective board minutes do not reproduce every remark made around the table. Instead, they establish a clear evidentiary record across four elements:

- **Information before the board:** Which papers, financial schedules, legal opinions, or management reports were circulated and reviewed prior to the meeting.
- **Material risks and alternatives weighed:** A concise summary of the key questions raised by non-executive directors, regulatory constraints noted by counsel, and management's responses.
- **Conflict disclosures and recusals:** Explicit notation under the Companies and Allied Matters Act (CAMA) 2020 when a director declares an interest and steps back from deliberation or voting.
- **Unambiguous resolutions:** Exact wording of each resolution, delegated authority limits, responsible officers, and reporting deadlines.

## Consistency Across Committees and Subsidiary Boards

Problems rarely arise in isolation at the parent board level. Audit, risk, and remuneration committees frequently examine issues in detail before referring recommendations upward. Where committee minutes and full board minutes contradict one another—or omit the link between a committee recommendation and a final board resolution—the governance record becomes vulnerable.

A disciplined company secretariat treats the board pack, committee reports, and statutory minute books as a single audit trail. That consistency protects both the company and individual directors long after the meeting has adjourned.
